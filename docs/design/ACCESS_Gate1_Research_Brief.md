# ACCESS — Gate 1 Research Brief (v0.1)
Date: 24 October 2026
Status: Discovery in progress; not approved for Gate 2

## Product framing
Access is a proposed shared communication experience for Deaf and hearing people in Kenyan hospitality workplaces. Initial scope: speech/text input, supported KSL output, and accessible response and clarification mechanisms. Avoid implying unrestricted translation.

## Central research question
How might Access enable Deaf and hearing people to communicate naturally, independently and confidently in hospitality workplaces without requiring either person to master the other's communication method?

## Stakeholders
- Deaf hospitality employees (primary co-design participants)
- Hearing coworkers and customers
- Supervisors and business operators
- KSL linguists/interpreters and Deaf community organizations

## Hypotheses to test
H01 Initiating conversations causes friction.
H02 Deaf employees carry disproportionate communication burden.
H03 Shared devices are practical in some settings.
H04 Verified routine hospitality phrases can be valuable.
H05 Users need to inspect/correct system interpretation.
H06 Sign avatar preferred to text for some users/contexts.
H07 Task-aware interface can outperform generic translator.
H08 Independence matters more than novelty.

## Research protocol
Recruit a range of Deaf KSL users with different language preferences, hearing staff and customers. Obtain informed consent, ensure interpretation is accessible, avoid recording without permission, and compensate participants where feasible.

### Contextual observation
Observe greetings, order-taking, directions, requests, clarification, and handoffs. Capture environment, device use, time pressure, privacy constraints and current workarounds. Do not record identifying guest data.

### Deaf employee interview prompts
1. Walk me through a typical customer interaction.
2. How does a hearing person get your attention?
3. Show me how you currently communicate complex requests.
4. Which interactions are easy, tiring or risky?
5. When has technology helped or made things harder?
6. What is your preferred language and communication mode in different contexts?
7. What would make a signing display easy to understand?
8. What would you want to control, correct or stop?
9. Which information should never be stored?
10. What would make you choose not to use Access?

### Hearing colleague/customer prompts
1. How do you initiate communication today?
2. What happens if a message is misunderstood?
3. How long are you comfortable waiting for an exchange?
4. Would you use a shared screen, and under what conditions?
5. What makes a successful interaction?

### Manager prompts
1. Which tasks have highest frequency or risk?
2. What devices, connectivity and policies are available?
3. What training, privacy and operating constraints exist?
4. What evidence would justify a pilot?

## Research capture template
Participant code | role | preferred language | task | trigger | current steps | workaround | breakdown | quote | observed vs reported | severity | frequency | opportunity | confidence | consent status

## Competitive audit rubric (1–5, evidence required)
- Start conversation: 15%
- Signing comprehension: 20%
- Two-way communication: 15%
- Speed/continuity: 15%
- Recovery: 15%
- Accessibility/control: 10%
- Hospitality fit: 10%
Do not score missing evidence as zero. Track tested device, date, version and source.
